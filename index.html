Hindu property division estimator

Estimate each heir's share of a Hindu male's estate under the Hindu Succession Act, 1956 (as amended in 2005), for Mitakshara and Dayabhaga families.

This tool gives algorithmic estimates based on statutory provisions and standard judicial interpretations. It is not legal advice or formal legal counsel. Wills, customs, local law, Section 15 (female owners), religion-specific law and disqualifications are not modelled. Consult an advocate before acting on any figure.

1. Legal framework

  • Self-acquired property: devolves as intestate succession among Class I heirs (Sections 8 and 10).
  • Ancestral, Mitakshara: a notional partition just before death gives the deceased his coparcenary share; daughters are coparceners by birth (Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma, 2020). That share then passes under Section 8.
  • Ancestral, Dayabhaga: sons acquire no birth right. The whole estate passes by inheritance in fixed fractions under Section 8.

3. Property and asset inventory

Divisible assets are land, plots, cash, bank balances and shares. Indivisible assets are cars, livestock, furniture, appliances and clothing.

Impartible property exceptions. Tick "Impartible" on any asset that is legally or customarily impartible (family deity or temple, genealogical records, rare books, unique ancestral jewellery, works of art, historic weapons, small houses that cannot be split without destroying their use, running businesses, religiously dedicated property, or anything that would lose substantial value if split). Such assets are never divided physically. Their value still counts toward each heir's entitlement, and the estimate shows who is compensated from other assets.
